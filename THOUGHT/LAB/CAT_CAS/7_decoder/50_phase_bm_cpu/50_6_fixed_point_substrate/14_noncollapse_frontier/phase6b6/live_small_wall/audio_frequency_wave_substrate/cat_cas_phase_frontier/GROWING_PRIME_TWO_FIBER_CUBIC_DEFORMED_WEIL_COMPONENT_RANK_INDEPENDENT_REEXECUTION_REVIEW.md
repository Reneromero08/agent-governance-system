## Resource law, comparison, and ceiling

The accepted Gaussian-component chart is exact but not a compact growing-order
non-Gaussian closure. One cubic intersection produces at least `(q-3)^2`
nonzero Weyl components in every declared order. A fully supported resident
two-port chart needs `3*q^2+19` field cells, and the disclosed conservative
two-map cubic-update peak is `6*q^2+q+19` field cells. Python object, allocator,
interpreter, and native-library memory remains excluded and is stated as such.

The strongest executed compact classical comparison applies the identical
public operator word to one q-vector per final probe. It retains `2*q` dynamic
field cells plus the counted `12*depth` public plan and no dynamic relation
state. Dense relation matrices are qualification oracles, not the matched
baseline. Thus this result identifies quadratic component growth relative to
an executed linear-state final-boundary recurrence; it does not establish a
phase resource or advantage.

The result is limited to the nine declared safe-prime pairs, two fibers, the
two public descriptor schedules, primary depth at most 4, alternate depth 3,
separable cubic phases, the declared four-probe boundary, and direct-process
Linux software. It does not establish exact full `q^2` saturation, a general
cubic Weil compiler, subquadratic non-Gaussian component support, fixed-width
unbounded state, CATVM custody, a distinct phase resource, computational
advantage, a Small Wall crossing, physical waveform execution, replacement of
physical bits with pi, or unbounded catalytic computation.
