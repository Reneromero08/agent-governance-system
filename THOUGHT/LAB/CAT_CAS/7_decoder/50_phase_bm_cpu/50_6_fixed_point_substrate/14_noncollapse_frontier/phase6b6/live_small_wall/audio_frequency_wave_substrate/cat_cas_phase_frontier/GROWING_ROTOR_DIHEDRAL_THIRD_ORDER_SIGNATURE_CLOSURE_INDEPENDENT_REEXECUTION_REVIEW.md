non-dihedral homometric bracelet pairs.  Eight coordinate pairs do.  The

2277` for rotor counts two through six.  This equals the dihedral bracelet
count at every declared rotor count.  It repairs transition closure without
retaining an explicit bracelet identifier, but it does not compact below
bracelet dimension at six rotors.

## Restoration and controls

Over F103 and F239, the depth-three primary transaction projects one final
scalar, recomputes the public word, subtracts it from the same target backing,
and restores every discrete field cell exactly.  The same source and target
allocations then execute a different depth-two program.  Fresh and restored
reuse boundaries agree, generation reaches two, and no snapshot or baseline
reload is used.

Missing, wrong, and reordered inverses leave nonzero residual cells for both
primes.  Null carriers are rejected.  This is exact algebraic restoration of
the two-register shear; it is not inversion of the generally noninvertible
scattering word.

## Claim ceiling

The result is limited to Grid17, exchange-symmetric, global-rotation and
reflection-invariant refined-signature inputs, topology cases at rotor counts
two through six, and R6 transactions over F103/F239 at primary depth three
and reuse depth two in direct-process software.

Preserved subclaims are strict two-coordinate minimality within the 33
observed triangle-count family, exact bracelet-orbit separation, all-shift
closure, final-only projection, exact same-backing restoration, unrelated
reuse, and independent particle-level parity.

Rejected interpretations include a refinement smaller than bracelet
identity, zero topology or plan cost, CATVM custody, a distinct phase
resource, computational advantage, Small Wall crossing, physical waveform
execution, physical replacement of bits with pi, and unbounded computation.
